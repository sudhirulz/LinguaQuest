The science of language
What is Linguistics?
Linguistics is the scientific study of language — how it is structured, how it works, how it changes over time, and how it varies across communities. You don't need to speak many languages to study linguistics; you need curiosity and a love of patterns.
Phonology
The sound systems of language — how sounds pattern and contrast across languages.
Morphology
Word structure — how words are built from smaller meaningful units called morphemes.
Syntax
Sentence structure — the rules governing how words combine into phrases and sentences.
Semantics
Meaning — how words and sentences convey what they mean.
Pragmatics
Language in use — how context shapes meaning in real communication.
Historical Linguistics
How languages change over time and how they are related to each other.

Competitive linguistics
Linguistics Olympiads
Problem-solving competitions where you decode unfamiliar languages using logic alone — no prior knowledge of that language needed. A problem might ask you to translate a sentence in Swahili, crack a writing system, or figure out the number system of an indigenous language.

"No knowledge of any language other than your own is required — just logical thinking and curiosity about how language works."

① You get data
A set of words, sentences, or examples from an unfamiliar language, with translations.
② You find patterns
Using the data, you work out the rules — grammar, sound changes, number systems, or script.
③ You apply them
Translate new sentences, decode new words, or crack a writing system using your rules.

How to Join India's IOL Team
India selects its IOL team through the Panini Linguistics Olympiad (PLO), organised by the Language Technologies Research Centre (LTRC) at IIIT Hyderabad.
1
Register for PLO
Open to all school and college students in India. Free, held online, typically January–February. No prior linguistics needed.

2
Training Camp at IIIT Hyderabad

Compete at IOL

Counting in Lutos

Given the positive multiples of 9 less than 100 in the Lutos language, decode the number system.

P1
Skills needed → Pattern recognition Base number systems Logical deduction Process of elimination
The Problem

Given below are the positive multiples of 9 that are smaller than 100, written in the Lutos language in random order.

ɓu zo m͡bakaɗɛunda so zo ɓu kal ɓuunda so zo ɓu zo kal ɓu unda ɗoi zo ɓu zo mi zo ɗoiunda so zo ɗoi unda ɗoi zo kal m͡bakaɗɛunda zio zo ɓu zo so unda muta zo ɓu zo ziounda zio zo mi unda muta zo muta

(a) Determine the correct correspondences between the Lutos words and the numbers 9, 18, 27, 36, 45, 54, 63, 72, 81, 90, 99.

Step-by-step Solution
1
Spot the pattern

9 out of 11 numbers start with unda. Only two don't — the two simplest are probably the smallest: 9 and 18.

2
Identify the base

The break at unda puts the base between 18 and 27. Let's try base 20.

💡 Base 20 (vigesimal) systems appear in Mayan, old French and Celtic languages. French quatre-vingts = "four twenties" = 80.
3
Count the groups

With base 20: 20–40 → unda doi, 40–60 → unda zio, 60–80 → unda muta, 80–100 → unda so. Three numbers in 80–100, so 80 = 20×4 → so = 4.

4
Crack the addition marker

zo appears between number-words — it's an addition connector.

unda so zo ɓu = 80+10 = 90 → ɓu = 10
unda so zo ɓu zo kal ɓu = 99 → kal ɓu = 9
5
Decode remaining words
kal = −1   m͡bakaɗɛ = 8   ɗoi = 1   mi = 5   zio = 2   muta = 3
💡 kal works as a subtractive prefix — like Latin undeviginti (19 = "one from twenty").
6
Final answers
9kal ɓu18ɓu zo m͡bakaɗɛ27unda ɗoi zo kal m͡bakaɗɛ36unda ɗoi zo ɓu zo mi zo ɗoi45unda zio zo mi54unda zio zo ɓu zo so63unda muta zo muta72unda muta zo ɓu zo zio81unda so zo ɗoi90unda so zo ɓu99unda so zo ɓu zo kal ɓu
Interesting Facts
🌍
Vigesimal systems are common

Base-20 counting appears in Mayan, Aztec, Basque, and old Celtic. French quatre-vingts (80) = "four twenties".

➖
Subtractive numerals

Latin undeviginti = 19 (one-from-twenty). Also in Danish, where 50 is "half-third-times-twenty".

🔗
zo as connector

Many languages use a word to link number components — English "and" in "a hundred and one", Arabic "wa".
